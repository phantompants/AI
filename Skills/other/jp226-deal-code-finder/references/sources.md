# Sources and search patterns

Sites change, close and rename. Treat this list as a starting point and drop any site that no longer works or looks poor quality. Add new ones as they are found.

## 1. OzBargain

The best first stop. It is a community site, so deals carry votes, dates and comments.

- Search: ozbargain.com.au, then search for the merchant name. Also try the merchant name followed by "coupon", "voucher" and "promo code".
- Look at the deal's posted date, the expiry date if one is set, and the vote count.
- Read the newest comments first. Comments such as "expired", "code not working" or "worked for me today" are the best test of whether a code is alive.
- Check the "coupons" and "deals" sections and any tag or store page that matches the merchant.
- Forum and competition pages sometimes hold codes too, but these are weaker leads.
- If a deal page links out, give the user the merchant's own page where possible, not the tracking link.

## 2. Other Australian coupon and deal sites

Use for a second opinion, and for codes OzBargain does not list. Check each is still operating. Examples of the kind of site to look for:

- Coupon aggregators for Australia, such as Cuponation, RetailMeNot Australia and similar.
- Price comparison and finance sites that run coupon pages, such as Finder.
- Group buying and travel deal sites, for holiday stays and attractions.
- Browser extension coupon pages, which list codes people have submitted.

Cautions: many aggregator pages list the same old code over and over. A code with no date, or a "reveal code" button that leads to a sign-up, is low confidence. Pages that promise a code for every merchant are usually generated and should be skipped.

## 3. The merchant's own site

Often the real discount is here and not in a code at all.

- Offers, specials and deals pages.
- Last minute deals and midweek or off season rates.
- "Stay more, pay less" offers such as pay for four nights, stay five.
- Book direct benefits, which are common for holiday parks.
- Newsletter sign-up offers. Tell the user what the offer is. Do not sign up for them.
- Gift cards and vouchers sold by the merchant.
- Terms pages, to see what blocks a code from stacking.

## 4. Membership and group rates

These often beat public codes, so ask about them or mention them.

- Merchant loyalty or member programs, including free tiers.
- Motoring and travel clubs, retiree and seniors cards, pensioner and concession rates.
- Defence, emergency services, health worker, teacher and student discounts.
- Bank, credit card and insurer perks, and workplace benefit platforms.
- Holiday park groups and their reciprocal membership or club cards.

Say plainly which memberships the rate depends on, and that the user must already hold them.

## 5. Cashback and gift cards

These can sit on top of a code or stand in for one.

- Cashback sites for Australia. Check whether the merchant is listed and what the current rate is, and note that rates change and may be paid later.
- Discounted gift cards from reputable resellers or from supermarket and bank reward programs. Check the card can be used for the kind of purchase, such as online bookings.
- Make sure the user knows cashback and gift cards usually have their own terms, and may not combine with a code.

## 6. Query patterns

Short and specific. Replace [merchant] with the business name, trying common spellings.

- [merchant] coupon
- [merchant] promo code
- [merchant] discount code [year]
- [merchant] ozbargain
- [merchant] voucher
- [merchant] sale
- [merchant] member discount
- [merchant] cashback
- [merchant] gift card discount
- [merchant] offers (merchant's own site)

For holiday parks add the group or brand and the state or region only if the user gave one, for example "Big4 Gold Coast discount".

## 7. Reading a result

| Signal | Reading |
|---|---|
| Posted within the last month, recent comments say it worked | High confidence |
| Posted within three months, no recent comments | Medium confidence |
| Older than three months, or any recent "expired" comment | Low confidence, likely stale |
| No date at all | Low confidence |
| Code needs a sign-up or app download to reveal | Low confidence, say so |
| Staff, friends and family or referral code | Do not hand out |

Write the evidence date next to every code. Never round old evidence up to "current".

## 8. When nothing is found

Say so first. Then offer, in this order:

1. A merchant offer or off peak rate that already lowers the price.
2. A membership or concession rate the user may qualify for.
3. Cashback or a discounted gift card, with the caveats above.
4. Setting a reminder to check again nearer a known sale period, such as end of financial year, Black Friday and Cyber Monday, Boxing Day, or school holiday lead ups.
5. Asking the merchant directly. For bookings, a call or message to the park or reservations team can sometimes get an extra night or a small discount.
